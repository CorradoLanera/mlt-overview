# Rubric — Item 02 (Long-range dependency: RNN traversal vs Transformer self-attention)

**Item**: IB-07-llm-transformers-02
**Objective**: Contrast the Transformer's self-attention with the RNN's sequential hidden state on a sequence of length $T_x$ — identify a long-range dependency in a clinical sentence, quantify how many intermediate hidden states an RNN must traverse to propagate that dependency, and locate the single entry of $Q K^{\top}$ that captures the same dependency in one step (Obj. 2).

The rubric scores three criteria, one per sub-question, on three levels — *Base* (sufficiency floor), *Good* (solid command), *Excellent* (effortless command + the *parallel-vs-serial* insight made explicit).

| Criterion | Base | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — Long-range dependency identification (sub-q 1)** | Identifies the pair *rules / out* (position $5$ or $6$) and *demonstrates / paroxysmal* (position $31$ or $32$) — i.e. names words from the *negation* half of the sentence and from the *assertion* half. The *position indices* are stated. May fail to articulate explicitly why *atrial$_{7}$ / paroxysmal$_{32}$* is the wrong pair. | All of *Base*, plus the **logical asymmetry** is named explicitly — the early word says **no** (the discharge summary excludes), the late word says **yes** (the Holter contradicts), and the clinician's question depends on resolving this contradiction. The student does **not** confuse *topic relatedness* with *logical dependency*. | All of *Good*, plus the student notes — even in one sentence — that this is the **same kind of structure** as the *denies $\leftrightarrow$ contradicts* dependency of *Sentence S* in the chapter's summative (the structural archetype, not the words): early *negation*, late *contradiction*, *long-range* in between. Recognising the *class of dependency*, not just this instance, signals chapter-level fluency. |
| **C2 — RNN traversal count (sub-q 2)** | Arrives at the **correct count $\mathbf{26}$** (or, equivalently, $25$ intermediate hidden states — *both* are acceptable, provided the convention is internally consistent and the count matches that convention) and shows the **calculation** explicitly: the two position indices $5$ and $31$, the subtraction $31 - 5$, the result $26$. A bare "$26$" without showing $31 - 5$ is **below Base** (it might be a memorised heuristic; the chapter is about *operating the count*, not recalling a number). | All of *Base*, plus the student **names** what each application of the recurrence *is* operationally — i.e. one application of $a^{<t>} = g(W_{aa} a^{<t-1>} + W_{ax} x^{<t>} + b_a)$, *not* just "a step" in vague terms. The recurrence formula appears, even if only by reference. | All of *Good*, plus the student **connects the count to fade** — notes, even in one sentence, that each of the $26$ applications is *one occasion for the signal of "rules out" to mix with the contributions of the intervening words* and therefore *one occasion for the dependency to be lost*. Stating *why the count matters* in terms of signal propagation is the chapter's central narrative beat for the RNN side. |
| **C3 — Transformer attention: matrix entry + sensitivity (sub-q 3)** | Identifies **one specific entry** of $Q K^{\top}$ — either $(31, 5)$ or its symmetric counterpart $(5, 31)$ — with both row and column indices explicit. **States the count of operations as $1$** (one inner product, one matrix entry). The bare "one entry" without specifying *which* row and column is **below Base** — the pedagogical point is that the entry has a *location*, not that it exists in the abstract. | All of *Base*, plus the student **uses the chapter's convention correctly** — names *query* and *key* by name, and states which position is the query and which is the key. (Either direction is acceptable: $(31, 5)$ with $q_{31}$ querying $k_5$ is the natural reading; $(5, 31)$ with $q_5$ querying $k_{31}$ is also acceptable.) | All of *Good*, plus the student **handles the sensitivity question** — states correctly that if the same two words were placed at positions $1$ and $1000$ in a sentence of length $T_x = 1000$, the **count would still be $1$ for the Transformer** (a single inner product, regardless of distance), while the **RNN count would grow to $999$**. Naming this **$1$-vs-$T_x - k$ asymmetry** is the chapter's payoff insight — the student has *internalised* why the architecture replaces the RNN, not just memorised that it does. |

**Soglia di sufficienza**: **tutti e tre C1, C2 e C3 a livello Base o superiore**. Identificare le due parole sbagliate (C1 < Base), saltare la sottrazione $31 - 5$ (C2 < Base), o non specificare riga e colonna dell'entry $Q K^{\top}$ (C3 < Base) equivale a non aver svolto l'esercizio: l'item misura la *capacità di operare* il confronto serial-vs-parallel su una frase concreta — i passaggi *visibili* sono lo strumento di misura.

**Bias da evitare in correzione:**

- **Penalizzare la scelta di $(5, 31)$ vs $(31, 5)$.** Entrambe sono accettabili: self-attention calcola *tutti* i prodotti scalari $\langle q_i, k_j \rangle$, e per una dipendenza simmetrica (non causale, non-masked) entrambe le entries codificano lo stesso pairing. La convenzione su quale sia "la" entry dipende da quale posizione si considera la *destinazione* del flusso informativo; lo studente che giustifica la scelta in modo coerente *non va penalizzato*.
- **Non confondere "ha contato bene" con "ha capito".** Uno studente che scrive "$31 - 5 = 26$" senza nominare la ricorrenza $a^{<t>} = g(W_{aa} a^{<t-1>} + W_{ax} x^{<t>} + b_a)$ resta a *Base* in C2; per arrivare a *Good* serve nominare *cosa* la ricorrenza *fa* in ciascun passaggio. La differenza fra *Base* e *Good* è la differenza fra *eseguire il calcolo* e *sapere cosa il calcolo rappresenta*.
- **Non penalizzare le formulazioni equivalenti del count.** "$26$ applicazioni della ricorrenza" e "$25$ stati intermedi" sono *la stessa cosa* a meno di una convenzione sull'inclusione/esclusione del punto di destinazione. Entrambe sono accettabili a *Base*, purché lo studente sia internamente coerente.
- **CRITICO — non scambiare *topic-relatedness* per *long-range dependency*.** Lo studente che identifica *atrial$_{7}$* e *paroxysmal$_{32}$* come "le due parole legate dalla dipendenza" ha trovato il *topic* (entrambe descrivono un tipo di disturbo cardiaco) ma ha **mancato la struttura logica della domanda** (chi afferma vs chi contraddice). Questa è la confusione tipica fra *attenzione semantica generica* e *attenzione richiesta dalla domanda*, ed è esattamente l'errore che il sommativo di [Sentence S](../objectives.md) usa come distrattore del Poll 1 *(penicillin, $2019$)*. **Riconoscere questo errore come C1 < Base è fondamentale**: passarlo come "abbastanza vicino" significa accettare uno studente che non ha capito *che cosa* il Transformer deve fare per rispondere alla domanda.
- **Non premiare la prosa sopra il calcolo.** Una risposta articolata e ben scritta che però non mostra né $31 - 5$ in C2 né la coppia di indici $(31, 5)$ in C3 non va sopra *Base* indipendentemente da quanto sia eloquente. L'item misura *operatività*, non *eloquenza*.
