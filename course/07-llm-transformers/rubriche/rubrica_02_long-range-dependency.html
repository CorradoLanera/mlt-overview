Rubric — Item 02 (Long-range dependency: RNN traversal vs Transformer self-attention)

Item: IB-07-llm-transformers-02 Objective: Contrast the Transformer's self-attention with the RNN's sequential hidden state on a sequence of length TxT_x — identify a long-range dependency in a clinical sentence, quantify how many intermediate hidden states an RNN must traverse to propagate that dependency, and locate the single entry of QK⊤Q K^{\top} that captures the same dependency in one step (Obj. 2).

The rubric scores three criteria, one per sub-question, on three levels — Base (sufficiency floor), Good (solid command), Excellent (effortless command + the parallel-vs-serial insight made explicit).

Criterion Base Good Excellent
C1 — Long-range dependency identification (sub-q 1) Identifies the pair rules / out (position 55 or 66) and demonstrates / paroxysmal (position 3131 or 3232) — i.e. names words from the negation half of the sentence and from the assertion half. The position indices are stated. May fail to articulate explicitly why atrial7_{7} / paroxysmal32_{32} is the wrong pair. All of Base, plus the logical asymmetry is named explicitly — the early word says no (the discharge summary excludes), the late word says yes (the Holter contradicts), and the clinician's question depends on resolving this contradiction. The student does not confuse topic relatedness with logical dependency. All of Good, plus the student notes — even in one sentence — that this is the same kind of structure as the denies ↔\leftrightarrow contradicts dependency of Sentence S in the chapter's summative (the structural archetype, not the words): early negation, late contradiction, long-range in between. Recognising the class of dependency, not just this instance, signals chapter-level fluency.
C2 — RNN traversal count (sub-q 2) Arrives at the correct count 𝟐𝟔\mathbf{26} (or, equivalently, 2525 intermediate hidden states — both are acceptable, provided the convention is internally consistent and the count matches that convention) and shows the calculation explicitly: the two position indices 55 and 3131, the subtraction 31−531 - 5, the result 2626. A bare "2626" without showing 31−531 - 5 is below Base (it might be a memorised heuristic; the chapter is about operating the count, not recalling a number). All of Base, plus the student names what each application of the recurrence is operationally — i.e. one application of a<t>=g(Waaa<t−1>+Waxx<t>+ba)a^{<t>} = g(W_{aa} a^{<t-1>} + W_{ax} x^{<t>} + b_a), not just "a step" in vague terms. The recurrence formula appears, even if only by reference. All of Good, plus the student connects the count to fade — notes, even in one sentence, that each of the 2626 applications is one occasion for the signal of "rules out" to mix with the contributions of the intervening words and therefore one occasion for the dependency to be lost. Stating why the count matters in terms of signal propagation is the chapter's central narrative beat for the RNN side.
C3 — Transformer attention: matrix entry + sensitivity (sub-q 3) Identifies one specific entry of QK⊤Q K^{\top} — either (31,5)(31, 5) or its symmetric counterpart (5,31)(5, 31) — with both row and column indices explicit. States the count of operations as 11 (one inner product, one matrix entry). The bare "one entry" without specifying which row and column is below Base — the pedagogical point is that the entry has a location, not that it exists in the abstract. All of Base, plus the student uses the chapter's convention correctly — names query and key by name, and states which position is the query and which is the key. (Either direction is acceptable: (31,5)(31, 5) with q31q_{31} querying k5k_5 is the natural reading; (5,31)(5, 31) with q5q_5 querying k31k_{31} is also acceptable.) All of Good, plus the student handles the sensitivity question — states correctly that if the same two words were placed at positions 11 and 10001000 in a sentence of length Tx=1000T_x = 1000, the count would still be 11 for the Transformer (a single inner product, regardless of distance), while the RNN count would grow to 999999. Naming this 11-vs-Tx−kT_x - k asymmetry is the chapter's payoff insight — the student has internalised why the architecture replaces the RNN, not just memorised that it does.

Soglia di sufficienza: tutti e tre C1, C2 e C3 a livello Base o superiore. Identificare le due parole sbagliate (C1 < Base), saltare la sottrazione 31−531 - 5 (C2 < Base), o non specificare riga e colonna dell'entry QK⊤Q K^{\top} (C3 < Base) equivale a non aver svolto l'esercizio: l'item misura la capacità di operare il confronto serial-vs-parallel su una frase concreta — i passaggi visibili sono lo strumento di misura.

Bias da evitare in correzione:

  • Penalizzare la scelta di (5,31)(5, 31) vs (31,5)(31, 5). Entrambe sono accettabili: self-attention calcola tutti i prodotti scalari ⟨qi,kj⟩\langle q_i, k_j \rangle, e per una dipendenza simmetrica (non causale, non-masked) entrambe le entries codificano lo stesso pairing. La convenzione su quale sia "la" entry dipende da quale posizione si considera la destinazione del flusso informativo; lo studente che giustifica la scelta in modo coerente non va penalizzato.
  • Non confondere "ha contato bene" con "ha capito". Uno studente che scrive "31−5=2631 - 5 = 26" senza nominare la ricorrenza a<t>=g(Waaa<t−1>+Waxx<t>+ba)a^{<t>} = g(W_{aa} a^{<t-1>} + W_{ax} x^{<t>} + b_a) resta a Base in C2; per arrivare a Good serve nominare cosa la ricorrenza fa in ciascun passaggio. La differenza fra Base e Good è la differenza fra eseguire il calcolo e sapere cosa il calcolo rappresenta.
  • Non penalizzare le formulazioni equivalenti del count. "2626 applicazioni della ricorrenza" e "2525 stati intermedi" sono la stessa cosa a meno di una convenzione sull'inclusione/esclusione del punto di destinazione. Entrambe sono accettabili a Base, purché lo studente sia internamente coerente.
  • CRITICO — non scambiare topic-relatedness per long-range dependency. Lo studente che identifica atrial7_{7} e paroxysmal32_{32} come "le due parole legate dalla dipendenza" ha trovato il topic (entrambe descrivono un tipo di disturbo cardiaco) ma ha mancato la struttura logica della domanda (chi afferma vs chi contraddice). Questa è la confusione tipica fra attenzione semantica generica e attenzione richiesta dalla domanda, ed è esattamente l'errore che il sommativo di Sentence S usa come distrattore del Poll 1 (penicillin, 20192019). Riconoscere questo errore come C1 < Base è fondamentale: passarlo come "abbastanza vicino" significa accettare uno studente che non ha capito che cosa il Transformer deve fare per rispondere alla domanda.
  • Non premiare la prosa sopra il calcolo. Una risposta articolata e ben scritta che però non mostra né 31−531 - 5 in C2 né la coppia di indici (31,5)(31, 5) in C3 non va sopra Base indipendentemente da quanto sia eloquente. L'item misura operatività, non eloquenza.
