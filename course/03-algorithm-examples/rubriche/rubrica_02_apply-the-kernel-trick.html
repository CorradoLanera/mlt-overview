Rubric — Item 02 (Apply the kernel trick)

Item: IB-03-algorithm-examples-02 Learning objective: Explain the kernel trick operationally — a non-linear feature map ϕ\phi can turn a non-linearly-separable dataset into a linearly-separable one without changing the linear classifier itself (Obj. 2).

Criterion Base Good Excellent
C1 — Non-separability argument (sub-question 1) Student asserts that no straight line works, but the reason is intuitive ("the classes overlap" / "they're mixed") without explicit reference to the concentric geometry. Student names the concentric structure — every direction contains both a red and a blue at different radii — as the obstruction, so any straight line has both classes on the same side. All of Good, plus identifies that the boundary the data requires is a closed curve (circle) around the origin, which by construction no straight line can be.
C2 — Apply ϕ\phi correctly to the 8 patients (sub-question 2) At least 5 of 8 transformations correct (i.e. X1=y12X_1 = y_1^2 and X2=y22X_2 = y_2^2 computed correctly), arithmetic visible. At least 7 of 8 correct; the symmetry between ±1\pm 1 and ±3\pm 3 collapsing to the same ϕ\phi-image is noticed and reported (reds collapse to {(1,0),(0,1)}\{(1,0), (0,1)\}, blues to {(9,0),(0,9)}\{(9,0), (0,9)\}). All 8 correct, and the student explicitly notes that X1+X2=y12+y22X_1 + X_2 = y_1^2 + y_2^2 is the squared distance from the origin, so all reds share X1+X2=1X_1 + X_2 = 1 and all blues share X1+X2=9X_1 + X_2 = 9.
C3 — Provide a linear separator and verify (sub-question 3) A linear rule is given (e.g. X1+X2<cX_1 + X_2 < c for some cc between 11 and 99), but the verification is missing or shown only on one class. A linear rule with specific values of a,b,ca, b, c is given, and verification is shown on at least one red and one blue, and the arithmetic is correct. All of Good, plus the student observes that any c∈(1,9)c \in (1, 9) works — i.e. there is a range of correct separators (a "margin") — and that all reds collapse to the line X1+X2=1X_1 + X_2 = 1, all blues to X1+X2=9X_1 + X_2 = 9, making the separation maximally easy.
C4 — What changed? (sub-question 4) Student says "the data changed" or "the coordinates changed" but does not explicitly state that the classifier itself (the straight cut) is unchanged. Student articulates that the classifier is unchanged — still a straight cut — and that what changed is the coordinates in which the data are expressed; this is enough for linear separation in the new space. All of Good, plus the student identifies that the linear separator X1+X2<cX_1 + X_2 < c in ϕ\phi-space corresponds to the circle y12+y22<cy_1^2 + y_2^2 < c in original space — i.e. a linear cut in the new coordinates is a curved boundary in the old. (Bonus: names this as the operational content of the kernel trick.)

Soglia di sufficienza: almeno 3 criteri su 4 a livello Base (e nessuno sotto Base). Below-Base in C2 = fewer than 5 transformations correct or arithmetic invisible; below-Base in C4 = does not address the "what changed" question (the conceptual gem — leaving it blank is not Base).

Bias da evitare in correzione:

  • Penalizzare la scelta di un cc diverso da 55 in C3 se la verifica è corretta. Qualsiasi c∈(1,9)c \in (1, 9) funziona; lo studente che sceglie c=4c = 4 o c=7c = 7 ha pienamente compreso il punto. Solo c=1c = 1 o c=9c = 9 (sui bordi, non strettamente separatori) o c≤0c \le 0 / c≥10c \ge 10 (separatori inesistenti) sono sbagliati.
  • Pretendere notazione specifica per la feature map o per il prodotto interno. Lo studente può chiamare X1,X2X_1, X_2 con qualsiasi nome (ad es. "u,vu, v" o "le coordinate trasformate"); l'importante è la sostanza.
  • Premiare la lunghezza in C4. Una risposta concisa e corretta ("the classifier is the same straight cut; what changed are the coordinates: in (X1,X2)(X_1, X_2) space the squared distances make linear separation trivial, and a line in ϕ\phi-space is the circle y12+y22=cy_1^2+y_2^2=c in original space") merita Excellent anche in 2 frasi. Una risposta lunga ma evasiva su cosa sia rimasto invariato è al più Base.
  • Penalizzare lo studente che usa l'aritmetica (−1)2=1(-1)^2 = 1 implicitamente in C2. Vale come arithmetic visible: il punto della consegna è applicare ϕ\phi, non scrivere (−1)⋅(−1)=1(-1) \cdot (-1) = 1. Se la tabella mostra i valori corretti, la traccia aritmetica è sufficiente.
  • Penalizzare lo studente che identifica ϕ\phi con un kernel polinomiale di grado 2.
