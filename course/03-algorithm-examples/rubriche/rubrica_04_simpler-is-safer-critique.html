Rubric — Item 04 (Which algorithm fits these data, and why?)

Item: IB-03-algorithm-examples-04 Learning objective: Argue, in terms of the shape of decision regions each algorithm can produce, which algorithm family fits a given dataset and which does not (Obj. 1 + light Obj. 2).

Criterion Base Good Excellent
C1 — Propose a fitting algorithm and name its boundary shape (Part 1) Student picks a chapter-03 algorithm (kernel SVM, kkNN, or RF) but does not explicitly name the shape of boundary it produces — only says "it will work" or "it can handle complex data". Student picks a chapter-03 algorithm and explicitly names the shape of boundary it produces — wiggly piecewise curve following the points for kkNN, smooth curved boundary for kernel SVM, axis-aligned staircase for RF — and argues that the shape matches the data (interlocked half-moons). All of Good, plus a mechanism-level connection: for kernel SVM, names a specific feature map ϕ\phi and explains that data become linearly separable in ϕ\phi-space; for kkNN, ties the wiggly boundary to the kk nearest training points voting on a new point's label; for RF, ties the staircase to the binomial vote concentrating on the right answer when p>0.5p > 0.5.
C2 — Identify a non-fitting algorithm and name its boundary shape (Part 2, first half) Student says linear SVM "will not work" but does not name a straight cut / single line as the boundary it produces — only says "it's too simple" or "the data are too complex". Student names linear SVM's boundary as a single straight line (f(x)=sgn(w⊤x+b)f(x) = \text{sgn}(w^\top x + b), a hyperplane) and argues that no straight line separates interlocked half-moons because every straight line crosses both classes. All of Good, plus the student observes that the failure is structural — a straight cut cannot produce the curved boundary the data require, regardless of how the line is positioned — not a contingent failure of a particular trained line.
C3 — Concrete consequence via 0/1 risk on the sample (Part 2, second half) Student says "linear SVM will misclassify many points" but does not invoke the 0/1 risk on the sample vocabulary from ch. 02. Student names high 0/1 risk on the sample as the consequence — the average 0/1 loss over the training patients is large because many fall on the wrong side of the straight cut. All of Good, plus the student observes that the high risk holds for any linear SVM trained on these data — even the best straight cut achieves only a poor 0/1 risk on interlocked half-moons (this is the structural sense of "no straight line works").

Soglia di sufficienza: almeno 2 criteri su 3 a livello Base (e nessuno sotto Base). Below-Base in C1 = picks linear SVM as fitting or picks a non-chapter-03 algorithm; below-Base in C2 = picks any of the curve-capable families (kkNN, kernel SVM, RF) as the non-fitting one; below-Base in C3 = does not connect to risk-on-sample vocabulary at all.

Bias da evitare in correzione:

  • Penalizzare il lessico di ch. 04 (generalisation, overfitting, cross-validation, model selection, regularisation, bias-variance trade-off) se la sostanza di ch. 03 è corretta. Lo studente che dice "linear SVM underfits" sta usando lessico di ch. 04 — corretto in generale ma fuori scopo. Tuttavia, se la sostanza (la forma è una retta, non matcha la shape, alto risk on sample) è correttamente articolata, vale Good o Excellent allo stesso modo. Non penalizzare la conoscenza dello studente; valuta solo la sostanza dell'argomento.
  • Pretendere il termine "inductive bias". Il capitolo non lo ha definito formalmente come termine che lo studente deve usare; il concetto è "la forma di confine che ogni algoritmo può disegnare". Uno studente che non scrive mai "inductive bias" ma parla correttamente di "the shape of boundary" è valutato esattamente come uno che usa il termine tecnico — vale solo la sostanza.
  • Premiare la menzione di più algoritmi in C1 senza una vera giustificazione. Se lo studente elenca "kernel SVM, kkNN, RF — tutti vanno bene" senza spiegare perché, valuta sull'argomento meglio sviluppato. Una giustificazione robusta su un algoritmo vale più di tre nomi senza argomento.
  • Dare ragione a chi sceglierebbe linear SVM "perché semplice". Il capitolo non offre la semplicità come criterio di scelta — offre il matching della forma alla shape dei dati. Se uno studente sceglie linear SVM su half-moons interlocked motivando "è il più semplice", è below-Base su C1: linear SVM non produce un confine che separi half-moons, qualunque sia il criterio di scelta.
