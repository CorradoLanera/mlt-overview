# Rubric — Item 02 (Apply the kernel trick)

**Item**: IB-03-algorithm-examples-02
**Learning objective**: Explain the kernel trick operationally — a non-linear feature map $\phi$ can turn a non-linearly-separable dataset into a linearly-separable one *without changing* the linear classifier itself (Obj. 2).

| Criterion | Base | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — Non-separability argument** (sub-question 1) | Student asserts that no straight line works, but the *reason* is intuitive ("the classes overlap" / "they're mixed") without explicit reference to the concentric geometry. | Student names the concentric structure — every direction contains both a red and a blue at different radii — as the obstruction, so any straight line has both classes on the same side. | All of Good, plus identifies that the boundary the *data* requires is a *closed curve* (circle) around the origin, which by construction no straight line can be. |
| **C2 — Apply $\phi$ correctly to the 8 patients** (sub-question 2) | At least **5 of 8** transformations correct (i.e. $X_1 = y_1^2$ and $X_2 = y_2^2$ computed correctly), arithmetic visible. | At least **7 of 8** correct; the symmetry between $\pm 1$ and $\pm 3$ collapsing to the same $\phi$-image is noticed and reported (reds collapse to $\{(1,0), (0,1)\}$, blues to $\{(9,0), (0,9)\}$). | **All 8** correct, *and* the student explicitly notes that $X_1 + X_2 = y_1^2 + y_2^2$ is the **squared distance from the origin**, so all reds share $X_1 + X_2 = 1$ and all blues share $X_1 + X_2 = 9$. |
| **C3 — Provide a linear separator and verify** (sub-question 3) | A linear rule is given (e.g. $X_1 + X_2 < c$ for some $c$ between $1$ and $9$), but the verification is missing or shown only on one class. | A linear rule with specific values of $a, b, c$ is given, **and** verification is shown on at least one red and one blue, **and** the arithmetic is correct. | All of Good, plus the student observes that any $c \in (1, 9)$ works — i.e. there is a *range* of correct separators (a "margin") — and that all reds collapse to the line $X_1 + X_2 = 1$, all blues to $X_1 + X_2 = 9$, making the separation maximally easy. |
| **C4 — What changed?** (sub-question 4) | Student says "the data changed" or "the coordinates changed" but does not explicitly state that the classifier itself (the straight cut) is unchanged. | Student articulates that **the classifier is unchanged — still a straight cut** — and that what changed is the *coordinates* in which the data are expressed; this is enough for linear separation in the new space. | All of Good, plus the student identifies that the linear separator $X_1 + X_2 < c$ in $\phi$-space corresponds to the **circle** $y_1^2 + y_2^2 < c$ in original space — i.e. *a linear cut in the new coordinates is a curved boundary in the old*. (Bonus: names this as the operational content of the kernel trick.) |

**Soglia di sufficienza:** **almeno 3 criteri su 4 a livello *Base*** (e nessuno sotto Base). Below-Base in C2 = fewer than 5 transformations correct *or* arithmetic invisible; below-Base in C4 = does not address the "what changed" question (the conceptual gem — leaving it blank is not Base).

**Bias da evitare in correzione:**

- **Penalizzare la scelta di un $c$ diverso da $5$ in C3 se la verifica è corretta.** Qualsiasi $c \in (1, 9)$ funziona; lo studente che sceglie $c = 4$ o $c = 7$ ha pienamente compreso il punto. Solo $c = 1$ o $c = 9$ (sui bordi, non strettamente separatori) o $c \le 0$ / $c \ge 10$ (separatori inesistenti) sono sbagliati.
- **Pretendere notazione specifica per la feature map o per il prodotto interno.** Lo studente può chiamare $X_1, X_2$ con qualsiasi nome (ad es. "$u, v$" o "le coordinate trasformate"); l'importante è la sostanza.
- **Premiare la lunghezza in C4.** Una risposta *concisa e corretta* ("the classifier is the same straight cut; what changed are the coordinates: in $(X_1, X_2)$ space the squared distances make linear separation trivial, and a line in $\phi$-space is the circle $y_1^2+y_2^2=c$ in original space") merita **Excellent** anche in 2 frasi. Una risposta lunga ma evasiva su *cosa* sia rimasto invariato è al più Base.
- **Penalizzare lo studente che usa l'aritmetica $(-1)^2 = 1$ implicitamente in C2.** Vale come arithmetic visible: il punto della consegna è applicare $\phi$, non scrivere $(-1) \cdot (-1) = 1$. Se la tabella mostra i valori corretti, la traccia aritmetica è sufficiente.
- **Penalizzare lo studente che identifica $\phi$ con un kernel polinomiale di grado 2.** Lo studente che dice "this is (a simplified) polynomial kernel of degree 2" è **corretto** (il chapter's example $\phi(y_1,y_2)=(y_1^2,y_2^2,\sqrt{2}y_1 y_2)$ è il kernel polinomiale completo; il nostro $\phi$ ne è una proiezione che basta per questa geometria). Non è leakage da ch. 04 — è connessione corretta al lessico stesso di ch. 03.
