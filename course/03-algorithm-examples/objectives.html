03 — Algorithm examples · Learning objectives & summative exercise

Chapter: Algorithm examples · 35 min · biomedical/clinical graduate students (UBEP). Student-facing text in English; teacher design notes (nota docente) in Italian.

Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, students can:

  1. Compare three algorithm families — kk-Nearest Neighbor, linear / Support-Vector Machine, and ensemble (Random Forest) — by the shape of the decision regions each one carves in the feature space: data-defined neighbourhoods around training points (kkNN), a maximum-margin hyperplane f(x)=sgn(w⊤x+b)f(x) = \text{sgn}(w^\top x + b) optionally through a kernel-induced feature map (SVM), or many axis-aligned cuts combined by majority vote (RF). Each shape is the kind of boundary that family of algorithm is capable of producing.
  2. Explain the kernel trick operationally: a non-linear feature map ϕ:ℝd→ℝD\phi:\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^D — e.g. (y1,y2)↦(y12,y22,2y1y2)(y_1, y_2) \mapsto (y_1^2,\ y_2^2,\ \sqrt{2}\,y_1 y_2) — can turn a non-linearly-separable dataset into a linearly-separable one, without changing the linear classifier itself; only what the classifier sees changes.
  3. Predict how the accuracy of a majority vote of mm independent weak classifiers with per-classifier accuracy pp behaves as mm and pp vary, and state why the construction only improves on a single classifier when p>0.5p > 0.5.

Nota docente: tre obiettivi per 35′ — uno per la forma delle regioni prodotte dalle tre famiglie, uno per il meccanismo trasversale (kernel), uno per il perché quantitativo del voto di maggioranza. I dettagli analitici di SVM (problema di ottimizzazione, hyper-parametro CC, scelta di kernel) e di RF (hyper-parametri mtry / ntree) restano voce docente: diventeranno oggetti di verifica al cap. 04, dove sono scelte di model selection. L'obiettivo 1 è la chiave dell'arco: stesso banco, tre algoritmi, tre forme di confine diverse. Il pre-hook al cap. 04 («ma quale vince su pazienti nuovi?») resta deliberatamente aperto a fine capitolo.

Summative live exercise — "Three algorithms, one dataset"

Nota docente: sommativo, gira negli ultimi ~10′ (min 24–34) dopo aver visto le tre famiglie e il kernel trick. Banco: una scatter 2D di ~12 pazienti su due misure cliniche (es. xx = fasting glucose, yy = BMI), due classi non-linearmente separabili — la geometria deve essere chiaramente non-lineare (es. mezzelune intrecciate, o cluster centrale di classe A circondato da classe B). La scelta non è cosmetica: solo se le classi NON sono linearmente separabili, le tre famiglie producono confini visibilmente diversi e l'obiettivo 1 è discriminante.

Field Content
Objective verified Obj. 1 (region shape per family) — verified per student through the predict-the-shape poll and the side-by-side board read-out. Obj. 2 (kernel trick) and Obj. 3 (majority vote) — verified at class level through the reveal: every student writes one sentence on the objective their group did not physically touch (see Timing / Success criterion — the 30″ all-class beat).
In-person action Three table groups (or six, doubled), each assigned one algorithm card: kkNN (k=3k=3), SVM (the group chooses linear or kernel), Random Forest (~50 trees). On the same printed scatter, each group: (i) sketches the decision boundary its algorithm would produce on this data — using pen on paper; (ii) writes a one-sentence answer to its algorithm-specific question — kkNN: "what happens to the boundary if kk is doubled?"; SVM: "this data is not linearly separable — do you pick linear or kernel, and which feature map would you use?"; RF: "5 trees each with p=0.6p=0.6, voted by majority — more or less accurate than 1 tree with p=0.6p=0.6? Why?". A group member photographs the sketch as soon as it is drawn and drops it into the shared doc.
Remote equivalent Same scatter on a shared slide with a drawing/annotation tool. Remote groups either draw the boundary directly or describe it in words ("piecewise, curving around each training point" / "smooth curve enclosing class A" / "axis-aligned staircase around each cluster") and type their algorithm-specific one-liner into their row of the shared doc. A Slido / poll collects the predicted region shape for each algorithm — options piecewise around points · smooth curve enclosing a class · axis-aligned staircase · single straight line · concentric circles (5 options for 3 algorithms, so the mapping is not trivially guessable by elimination) — so the remote vote is visible live.
Shared artifact A 3-column board (one column per algorithm: kkNN · SVM · RF), each column collecting all the sketches of that family — in-person photographs + remote drawings — side by side. The class sees three columns of boundary shapes at once.
Timing 10 min, minutes 24–34 (end of chapter): ~2′ read & assign, ~5′ sketch + algorithm-specific one-liner (photos uploaded during this phase so the board is already populated), ~30″ silent all-class beat — each student writes one sentence on the objective their group did not touch (kkNN group: the kernel-trick one-liner; SVM group: the majority-vote one-liner; RF group: the boundary-with-larger-kk one-liner) into a personal row of the shared doc, ~2′30″ instructor-led reveal — the three columns get named (piecewise / max-margin / vote-of-cuts) and the characteristic boundary shape of each family is read straight off the board, then 3 random "cross-objective" one-liners are read aloud.
Success criterion Each algorithm column produces a recognisable, family-correct boundary shape: kkNN → piecewise, curving around individual training points or small clusters of them; SVM → shape consistent with the group's own linear-vs-kernel choice: if linear, a straight line plus the one-liner must state explicitly why a straight line fails on this dataset (e.g. "the two classes are interleaved / one wraps the other, no straight line can separate them"); if kernel, a smooth curved boundary plus the one-liner must name the non-linear feature map; RF → axis-aligned "staircase" of many small rectangles. The three one-liners are correct: kkNN — "boundary gets smoother as kk grows" (averages over more neighbours); SVM — picks kernel and names a non-linear feature map (ϕ\phi that lifts to higher dimension); RF — "more accurate, because the majority vote concentrates on the right answer as long as each tree is better than chance (p>0.5p > 0.5)". Live signal of mastery: the class can point at the three columns and articulate — without prompting — that the data are the same; the algorithms differ; the shapes of boundary each one can produce differ → so the carved regions differ.

Nota docente — perché duale funziona qui: l'artefatto condiviso è uno solo (la lavagna a 3 colonne) e mostra in un colpo d'occhio l'idea centrale del capitolo: stesso banco, tre forme di regione diverse. Il poll Slido sulle region shape dà al docente un segnale aggregato e immediato anche dai remoti su Obj. 1 — altrimenti durante la fase di sketch i remoti restano osservatori muti (è l'unico canale potenzialmente asimmetrico, lo sketch su carta). Se la classe è piccola (< 3 gruppi), unisci kkNN e RF in un solo gruppo "carve-by-data" e tieni SVM come gruppo a parte: il confronto kernel-vs-non-kernel sopravvive comunque.

Nota docente — reveal e pre-hook al cap. 04: chiudi l'esercizio senza dichiarare vincitori. La domanda "quale di questi tre confini funziona meglio su pazienti nuovi?" deve restare aperta — è esattamente il gancio che il cap. 04 (overfitting, train/validation/test, cross-validation) raccoglie. Se un gruppo dichiara "il nostro è il più accurato", rispondi "su questi 12 pazienti, sì — ma su un paziente che non hai mai visto?" senza chiudere. È quel cliff-hanger a tenere la classe per il capitolo successivo.

Nota docente — sulla scelta degli hyperparametri nelle card: k=3k=3 per kkNN (la "rule of thumb" del capitolo), ~50 alberi per RF (sufficienti perché il voto concentri, manegevoli da nominare), e per SVM nessun default forzato — è proprio la scelta linear vs kernel la performance osservabile dell'obiettivo 2. Non chiedere CC, non chiedere mtry: sono il gancio del cap. 04 e qui sarebbero rumore.
