# Rubric — Item 02 (Compute a 5-fold CV error)

**Item**: IB-04-model-selection-02
**Learning objective**: Use $K$-fold cross-validation: compute the CV error rate from per-fold errors, identify what it estimates, and recognise the trade-off involved in choosing $K$ (Obj. 3).

| Criterion | Base | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — Compute the CV error rate** (sub-question 1) | The numerical result is correct ($30\%$) but the formula or the arithmetic step is missing — the student wrote the answer but did not show the *averaging* procedure. | Both formula and result correct: the student writes either $\frac{1}{K} \sum_k \text{error}_k$ or $\frac{\text{total misclassified}}{\text{total patients}}$ explicitly, plugs the numbers in, gets $30\%$. | All of Good, plus the student shows the **two equivalent computations** (average of per-fold rates *and* total-misclassified divided by total-patients) yielding the same result, and identifies *why* they agree (each fold has the same size, $4$ patients). |
| **C2 — State what the CV error estimates** (sub-question 2) | Student says "the error" or "the model's performance" without distinguishing it from training error and test error. | Student states explicitly that the CV error estimates the **prediction error on unseen patients** (or "patients drawn from the same population"), distinguishing it from training error. | All of Good, plus the student notes that the CV error is a **validation-stage** estimate — used to *choose* among candidate models — and is therefore *not* the same as the *test* error reported as the final number (recalling the train/val/test role split from Obj. 2). |
| **C3 — Trade-off when $K$ changes** (sub-question 3) | Student correctly describes the **procedure** for $K = 10$ (10 folds of 2 patients each, train on 18 + estimate on 2, repeat 10 times, average) but does not identify a trade-off or identifies a wrong/imprecise one. | Student describes the procedure correctly **and** identifies **one** of the two directions of the trade-off — *either* "more training data per round → less bias" *or* "smaller folds → noisier per-fold rates → more variance". | All of Good, plus the student names **both directions** of the trade-off (less bias *and* more variance) and ties them to why $K \in [5, 10]$ is the standard recommendation: the balance between *enough training data per round* and *enough patients per fold to make the rate meaningful*. |

**Soglia di sufficienza:** **almeno 2 criteri su 3 a livello *Base*** (e nessuno sotto Base). Below-Base in C1 = numerical result wrong (the procedure was not applied correctly); below-Base in C2 = student says "training error" or "test error" instead of "prediction error on unseen data"; below-Base in C3 = no description of the $K = 10$ procedure at all *or* a description that contradicts the procedure (e.g., "K=10 means run CV 10 times").

**Bias da evitare in correzione:**

- **Pretendere la formula in notazione di sommatoria per C1.** Uno studente che scrive *"average of the per-fold errors: $\frac{25 + 25 + 50 + 0 + 50}{5} = 30\%$"* senza la notazione $\sum$ ha mostrato la formula in modo equivalente; vale **Good**. Solo la *assenza* della media (i.e., risultato giusto ma senza nessuna traccia di averaging) abbassa a Base.
- **Penalizzare lo studente che dice "prediction error" senza nominare esplicitamente "*unseen patients*" in C2.** Se lo studente ha scritto *"the error the classifier would make on new data"* o *"on patients the model has not seen"*, la sostanza è corretta — vale **Good**. L'espressione canonica del capitolo ("on unseen data" / "prediction error") non è una formula da memorizzare.
- **Penalizzare in C3 chi parla di "more variance / less bias" in modo qualitativo, senza la quantificazione esatta.** Lo scopo del trade-off in ch. 04 è la *direzione qualitativa*, non la formalizzazione probabilistica del bias-variance trade-off (quella resta voce docente). Una risposta corretta in direzione qualitativa, anche senza formule, vale al pieno livello dichiarato.
- **Lessico di ch. 05 (deep learning, NN, layers).** Il capitolo non lo richiede. Uno studente che lo importa per dare un esempio (es. "for a deep network, $K=5$ vs $K=10$ would mean...") **non va premiato di più** per il lessico — vale solo la sostanza ch. 04 — ma neppure penalizzato se la sostanza è giusta.
- **Penalizzare $K = 10$ vs $K = 5$ in C3 se lo studente argomenta "$K = 10$ è sempre meglio".** È una posizione difendibile (più training data per round) ma **incompleta**: il chapter spinge sul trade-off. Va trattato come **Base** in C3 (riconosce la direzione bias, non la direzione variance), **non** Good.
