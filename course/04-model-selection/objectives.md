# 04 — Model selection & validation · Learning objectives & summative exercise

> Chapter: **Model selection & validation** · 30 min · biomedical/clinical graduate students (UBEP).
> Student-facing text in English; teacher design notes (*nota docente*) in Italian.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, students can:

1. **Diagnose** overfitting from the signature *low training error + high error on new data*: name the phenomenon, and state its cause — the model has captured idiosyncrasies of the training sample, not the underlying signal the task wants to learn — rather than a fault of the algorithm itself.
2. **Apply** the train / validation / test split to a model-selection task: assign each subset its role — *training* fits the model's parameters, *validation* chooses between candidate models / hyperparameter settings, *test* (held out) estimates the final model's performance on truly unseen data — and state explicitly why **the same data cannot be used both to choose a model and to estimate its performance** (every decision taken after looking at a number on a dataset contaminates that number as a future estimate).
3. **Use** $K$-fold cross-validation as the practical procedure for the *validation* step: state the algorithm — split the (non-test) data into $K$ equal blocks; for $k = 1, \ldots, K$ train on the remaining blocks and estimate error on block $k$; average the $K$ errors — identify what the CV error rate **estimates** (the prediction error of the chosen model on unseen data), and recognise that $K \in [5, 10]$ is the standard range (smaller $K$ over-trains, larger $K$ adds variance).

*Nota docente:* tre obiettivi per 30′. **Questo capitolo è il payoff narrativo dei cap. 02–03**: chiude la pushback di "Clinician A" (cap. 02 item 04 — "ma su pazienti nuovi?") e l'iperaderenza di $k$NN $k=1$ (cap. 03 storyboard frame 6, narrative pre-hook). I tre obiettivi sono uno per il *fenomeno* (Obj. 1, diagnostico), uno per la *struttura* della soluzione (Obj. 2, split + perché tre set), uno per il *metodo* canonico (Obj. 3, $K$-fold CV). Il **bias–variance trade-off** resta **voce docente**: è la cornice concettuale del *perché* la complessità del modello porta all'overfitting, ma non è una performance separatamente verificabile in 30′. La tabella *parameters vs hyper-parameters* per i vari algoritmi (decision tree / RF / SVM / logistic / ANN) resta esposizione, non valutazione. Il pre-hook al cap. 05 ("e se il modello può essere reso arbitrariamente complesso? — reti neurali profonde") va nella `narrative.md`, non qui.

## Summative live exercise — "Catch the overfitter"

*Nota docente:* sommativo, gira negli **ultimi ~10′** (min 20–30). **Banco:** quattro modelli candidati per uno stesso compito clinico (es. classificazione *high-risk / low-risk* su pazienti diabetici), tutti $k$NN con hyper-parametro $k$ diverso, valutati con **5-fold CV** sul training+validation set ($100$ pazienti). La tabella va proiettata e distribuita ai banchi / shared slide:

| Model | $k$ ($k$NN) | Training error | 5-fold CV error |
|---|---|---|---|
| $M_A$ | $k = 1$ | $0 / 100$ ($0\%$) | $30 / 100$ ($30\%$) |
| $M_B$ | $k = 5$ | $12 / 100$ ($12\%$) | $18 / 100$ ($18\%$) |
| $M_C$ | $k = 15$ | $20 / 100$ ($20\%$) | $22 / 100$ ($22\%$) |
| $M_D$ | $k = 51$ | $40 / 100$ ($40\%$) | $42 / 100$ ($42\%$) |

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Objective verified | Obj. 1 (diagnose overfitting), Obj. 2 (why a separate test set is still needed after CV picks), Obj. 3 (CV as the procedure to *select* among candidates). |
| In-person action | Table groups receive the 4-row table on paper. They: (i) **identify the overfitter** — the model with the largest gap between training error and CV error — and name *what* the phenomenon is and *why* it happens for that specific $k$; (ii) **pick the best model** by CV error and write its row down; (iii) **answer in one sentence**: *"You now have a winner from the CV table. Why is the CV error of that winner **not** a trustworthy estimate of its performance on new patients, and what data would you use to get a trustworthy estimate?"* — the answer must invoke the *every-decision-contaminates* rule from Obj. 2; (iv) **assign the role** of each of the three subsets by completing the three blanks: *"The TRAINING set is used to ___. The VALIDATION set (here implemented as 5-fold CV) is used to ___. The TEST set is used to ___."*. Each group photographs its answers and drops them into the shared doc. |
| Remote equivalent | Same table on a shared slide. Remote groups type their four answers directly into their row of the shared doc. A Slido / poll collects the **identified overfitter** (4 options $M_A$ / $M_B$ / $M_C$ / $M_D$) so the remote-vs-presence vote is visible live. |
| Shared artifact | A single shared doc / whiteboard with a 4-column section per group: *overfitter id + reason* · *winner id by CV* · *why CV ≠ test estimate* · *roles of the three subsets*. Visible to in-person and remote at once. |
| Timing | 10 min, minutes 20–30 (end of chapter): ~2′ read the table + assign, ~5′ groups fill in the four cells (photos uploaded *during* this phase), ~3′ instructor-led reveal — the four models are explicitly labelled (*$M_A$ overfit / $M_B$ sweet spot / $M_C$ smoother / $M_D$ underfit*); the test-set question is closed in front of the class; **and the instructor explicitly names the prior cliff-hangers that this very procedure resolves** — *"this is what was at stake when **Clinician A** asked us in ch. 02 (item 04) 'but on a different group of patients?' — we now have the answer; and this is why **$1$NN's paper-thin wrap** in ch. 03 (storyboard frame 6) was a trap — its zero training error was the very signature of $M_A$ in front of us."* |
| Success criterion | The class produces: (i) **$M_A$** correctly identified as the overfitter, *and* the reason given as *"$k=1$ memorises every training point — zero training error — but generalises badly on new data"* (or equivalent); (ii) **$M_B$** picked as the winner by CV ($18\%$ is the lowest CV error) — *note for the corrector: a group that picks $M_C$ "because it has the smallest training-vs-CV gap" has misread the criterion ('lowest CV', not 'smallest gap') and is a **teachable moment** in the reveal, not a fail*; (iii) the test-set answer invokes the rule that **once the CV error has been used to choose $M_B$, that very CV number is no longer an unbiased estimate of $M_B$'s performance on new patients** — a separate held-out **test set**, not used for selection, is needed for the final estimate; (iv) the three roles correctly assigned — *training → fits the model's parameters*, *validation (5-fold CV) → chooses among candidate models / hyperparameters*, *test → estimates final performance on truly unseen patients*. **Live signal of mastery:** the class can articulate without prompting that *training error alone is misleading*, *the CV error chose the model*, *the test error is the trustworthy number to report to the clinical team*, and *each subset has a single, non-substitutable role*. |

*Nota docente — perché duale funziona qui:* l'artefatto condiviso è **uno solo** (la tabella a 4 modelli), e tutto il sommativo è verbale/numerico — niente da disegnare, niente asimmetria presenza/remoto. Il poll Slido sul *overfitter id* dà al docente un segnale aggregato immediato anche dai remoti su Obj. 1; le tre celle nel doc condiviso danno la traccia osservabile per Obj. 2 e Obj. 3. Se la classe è piccola (< 4 gruppi), assegna una *seconda* domanda di reveal: *"se cambi $K$ da $5$ a $10$ nella CV, cosa ti aspetti accada alla tabella?"* — anticipazione del bias-variance della CV stessa, voce docente.

*Nota docente — sulla scelta dei numeri della tabella:* la **gap** training-vs-CV è il segnale di overfitting ($M_A$: $0\%$ vs $30\%$ = gap di $30$ punti). $M_B$ è il *winner* per CV ($18\%$) ma il suo training error ($12\%$) è già non zero — questo previene la confusione "best = lowest training error". $M_C$ ha training e CV vicini (entrambi $\sim 20\%$) ma più alti di $M_B$ — "no gap ma comunque non vincente" insegna che *l'assenza di overfitting non è sufficiente*. $M_D$ ($k = 51$) è il caso *underfit*: training error alto perché il modello è troppo rigido. Tieni questo come voce docente per il reveal — non lo chiedere nei gruppi, ma proponilo dopo come "e $M_D$? perché ha errore alto sia sul training sia in CV?" — apre il bias–variance.

*Nota docente — anti-trabocchetto sul "CV error ≠ test estimate":* lo studente medio del UBEP **sa già** che servono dati separati per test (intuizione clinica); la sottigliezza che ch. 04 aggiunge è il **perché**: ogni volta che usi un numero per *scegliere* qualcosa, quel numero diventa inutilizzabile come *stima*. Insegnerai questo nella consegna come la regola operativa "*every decision taken after looking at a number on a dataset contaminates that number as a future estimate*"; spingi lo studente nella sub-question 3 a esprimere *questa* idea, non la generica "servono più dati".
