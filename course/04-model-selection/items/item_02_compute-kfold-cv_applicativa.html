Item 02 — Compute a 5-fold cross-validation error

ID: IB-04-model-selection-02 Learning objective: Use KK-fold cross-validation as the practical procedure for the validation step: state the algorithm, compute the CV error rate from per-fold errors, identify what the CV error estimates, and recognise that K∈[5,10]K \in [5, 10] is the standard range (Obj. 3). Category: Application Type: applicativa (open-ended applicative) Difficulty: medium Chapter: 04-model-selection

Question

A classifier is evaluated on a dataset of 2020 patients (each labelled high-risk or low-risk) using 55-fold cross-validation. The dataset is divided into 55 equal folds of 44 patients each. For each fold, the classifier is trained on the other 44 folds (i.e., on 1616 patients) and evaluated on the held-out fold (the remaining 44 patients). The table reports the number of misclassified patients per held-out fold:

Fold Misclassified Out of Per-fold error rate
11 11 44 25%25\%
22 11 44 25%25\%
33 22 44 50%50\%
44 00 44 0%0\%
55 22 44 50%50\%

Answer the three sub-questions:

  1. Compute the 55-fold CV error rate for this classifier. Show the formula you used and report the result as a percentage.
  2. State what number the CV error rate estimates — i.e. what is it a number about? (1–2 sentences.)
  3. Describe what would change if we used K=10K = 10 instead of K=5K = 5 on the same 2020-patient dataset: (a) what the procedure would look like, and (b) one concrete trade-off involved in increasing KK.

Expected answer

  1. The 55-fold CV error rate is the average of the per-fold error rates (equivalently, the total misclassified patients divided by the total dataset size):

    CV error=1K∑k=1K(errork)=25%+25%+50%+0%+50%5=150%5=𝟑𝟎%.\text{CV error} \;=\; \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} (\text{error}_k) \;=\; \frac{25\% + 25\% + 50\% + 0\% + 50\%}{5} \;=\; \frac{150\%}{5} \;=\; \mathbf{30\%}.

    Equivalent computation: (1+1+2+0+2)/20=6/20=30%(1 + 1 + 2 + 0 + 2) / 20 = 6 / 20 = 30\%.

  2. The CV error rate estimates the prediction error of the classifier on unseen patients — i.e., the error rate one would expect if the classifier were applied to a new group of patients drawn from the same population. It is not the training error (which measures fit on already-seen data) and it is not yet the test error (which is reserved for the final, held-out test set after a model is chosen).

  3. With K=10K = 10 instead of K=5K = 5 on the same 2020 patients:

    (a) Procedure: divide the 2020 patients into 1010 equal folds of 22 patients each. For each of the 1010 folds, train the classifier on the remaining 99 folds (i.e., on 1818 patients) and estimate the error on the 22 held-out patients. Average the 1010 per-fold error rates to get the CV error.

    (b) Trade-off: with larger KK, each round trains on more data (1818 instead of 1616 patients per round), so each round's classifier is closer to one trained on the full dataset — less bias in the error estimate. But each fold is smaller (22 patients instead of 44): a single misclassified patient now flips the per-fold error rate by 5050 percentage points, so the per-fold rates are noisier and the average has more variance. This is why the chapter recommends K∈[5,10]K \in [5, 10] as the standard range — it balances "enough training data per round" (low bias) against "enough patients per fold to make the rate meaningful" (low variance).
