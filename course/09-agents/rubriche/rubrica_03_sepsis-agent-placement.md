# Rubric — Item 03 (Place the gates: sepsis early-warning agent)

**Item**: IB-09-agents-03
**Objective**: Apply the chapter's reversibility + human-in-the-loop test to each step of a multi-step clinical agent workflow, classify each step as *safe-by-default* or *dangerous-by-default*, and propose a gate placement that adds a guard exactly where reversibility fails — and nowhere else. Justify each gate with the chapter's operational rule (*ask before any irreversible action*) and explain why over-gating is not required (Obj. 3).

The rubric scores three criteria, one per sub-question, on three levels — *Base* (sufficiency floor), *Good* (solid command), *Excellent* (operational fluency that generalises beyond the specific scenario).

| Criterion | Base | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — Reversibility + HITL classification (sub-q a)** | At least **$3$ out of $4$** steps correctly classified on **both** axes (reversibility + HITL), **and** at least **one** of step $3$ or step $4$ correctly identified as *dangerous-by-default*. Each row's justification names the *agent's action* (read / compute / send-alert / page), not the *data's sensitivity* or *the consequence on the world*. **Below Base** *(critical)*: classifying step $1$ as *irreversible* because *"it accesses PHI"*, classifying step $2$ as *irreversible* because *"it computes a clinical score"*, or marking *both* of steps $3$ and $4$ as *reversible* — these are the canonical confusions the chapter's $30$-second test is designed to surface, and getting them wrong means the test has not been internalised. | All of *Base*, plus **all four** steps correctly classified on **both** axes (4/4 reversibility, 4/4 HITL), **and** both of steps $3$ and $4$ correctly identified as *dangerous-by-default*. The justifications for steps $1$ and $2$ explicitly distinguish *the agent's action* (read; in-memory compute) from *the world's record-keeping* (the EHR query log; nothing) — i.e. they pre-empt the *audit-log-feels-permanent* misreading. | All of *Good*, plus the justification for step $3$ explicitly recognises that the irreversibility of the HL7 alert is **simultaneous-broadcast irreversibility**: the agent's *write* (sending the HL7 message) and the alert's *propagation to bedside hardware* (the buzz firing on the nurse-call panel, the dashboard updating) happen in the **same operation** — the agent cannot separate the message-send from the buzz-firing or the dashboard-rendering. The student **contrasts this case explicitly with item 02 step 3** (CSV-on-shared-drive, *visible-but-reversible*): there the *visibility* is downstream of the action and the action itself (file write) is rollback-able by file deletion; here the *visibility* (bedside buzz) **is** the action's first-order effect and cannot be separated from the send. This is the chapter's *visible vs consequential* distinction sharpened to the *broadcast-on-write* case. |
| **C2 — Gate placement (sub-q b)** | At least **one** gate placed at the **correct position** (before step $3$ *or* before step $4$). **No** gates placed before steps $1$ or $2$ — over-gating before a reversible step is a placement error, not a *safer* choice. **Below Base** *(critical)*: gating **all four** steps *"for safety"* (the *paralyzingly conservative* placement the chapter's cap. 08 pre-hook explicitly warns against), or placing **zero** gates (the workflow as described would page the on-call without any human reading what or why), or placing a gate **only** after step $4$ (i.e. *post-hoc* review — the chapter's rule is *ask before*, not *review after*). | All of *Base*, plus **exactly two** gates, positioned **before step $3$** and **before step $4$**, with **no** gates before steps $1$ or $2$. The student has applied the rule **once per irreversible step**, not once per workflow, and has not collapsed the two dangerous steps into a single late gate (which would let the HL7 alert broadcast before the SMS is reviewed). | All of *Good*, plus a brief one-line note on **what each gate must show the human** before the action proceeds — for the HL7-alert gate, the *qSOFA value + patient identifier + the underlying vitals/labs the score is based on* (not just *"approve send?"*); for the SMS gate, *the patient identifier + the threshold-crossing fact* (not just *"send page?"*). This signals that the student has understood the gate as a *render-and-wait* operation (the chapter's *"pauses, renders the action it is about to take, and waits"* formulation), not as a generic confirm-button. |
| **C3 — Justification with the chapter's rule (sub-q c)** | Each gate placed in C2 is justified by **explicit reference** to the chapter's rule — either *"safe-by-default if reversible **or** human-in-the-loop"*, *"the dangerous quadrant is irreversible $\wedge$ no-HITL"*, or *"ask before any irreversible action"*. Vague justifications such as *"to be safe"*, *"because clinical"*, *"to protect the patient"* are **below Base** — they may name a *true motivation* but they are *not* the operational rule the chapter has taught. The justification must connect *this specific step's irreversibility* to *the rule that requires a gate here*. | All of *Base*, plus an **explicit one-sentence** on **why no gate is needed before steps $1$ or $2$**, citing the chapter's *safe-by-default if reversible* principle and naming over-gating as the failure mode the chapter warns against (*"a gate before a reversible step does not reduce risk; it paralyses the agent"*, or any equivalent operational formulation). The student is reasoning *symmetrically* — gates go where reversibility fails *and only there*. | All of *Good*, plus recognition that the chapter's rule **survives the introduction of a new tool**: a brief one-sentence noting that *if tomorrow the agent is configured with a fifth step* (e.g. *create a clinical order in the CPOE*, *write a discharge summary*, *call the rapid-response team*), the same rule (reversibility test → gate before irreversible) places the gate **without rewriting the policy**. This signals operational fluency: the student is reasoning about the *rule*, not about the *specific workflow*. The chapter's claim that the rule *"survives any new tool added tomorrow"* is the chapter-level fluency mark for Obj. 3, and naming it here puts the answer at Excellent. |

**Soglia di sufficienza**: **tutti e tre C1, C2 e C3 a livello Base o superiore**. Una classificazione di reversibilità sbagliata su step $1$ o step $2$ *che non corregge nessuno dei due dangerous (3 o 4)* è sotto Base. Una placement con zero gate o con gate solo su step reversibili è sotto Base. Una giustificazione *senza riferimento alla regola del cap. 09* (anche se accurata clinicamente) è sotto Base — l'item misura *l'applicazione della regola di trust engineering*, non la sensibilità clinica.

**Bias da evitare in correzione:**

- **CRITICO — non valutare la correttezza clinica del qSOFA.** L'item non misura se lo studente sa che il qSOFA è la *quick SOFA*, conosce le sue tre componenti (BP $\leq 100$, RR $\geq 22$, GCS $< 15$), o sa che la soglia $\geq 2$ è la soglia di alert standard. Lo studente che mostra anche fluenza clinica sul qSOFA è un bonus *fuori rubrica*; lo studente che non la mostra **non è penalizzato**. La rubrica misura *placement del gate* e *applicazione della regola*, non *clinical knowledge of qSOFA*. Se uno studente argomenta correttamente il gating senza nominare il qSOFA, è a Base/Buono/Eccellente sulla *trust engineering*, indipendentemente.
- **CRITICO — non premiare la conservatività paralizzante.** Uno studente che gata *tutti i quattro step* "per maggiore sicurezza clinica" non sta dando una risposta *più cauta*; sta dando una risposta *operativamente sbagliata*. Il chapter è esplicito (pre-hook di cap. 08): *"if you want a human reading every step, use a chat-LLM. Agents are the thing you reach for when the workflow has steps you don't need to read."* Un gate prima di ogni step **disfa l'agente** — è l'errore *paralyzingly conservative* nominato dal cap. 08, e il C2 lo classifica esplicitamente sotto Base. **Non scambiare conservatività per rigore.**
- **Non penalizzare lo studente che ammette ambiguità sullo step $3$ con argomentazione.** Un EHR write *tecnicamente* lo si può cancellare in $30$ secondi su alcuni sistemi (il void leaves audit-trail ma l'annotation può scomparire dalla vista clinica). Uno studente che scrive *"Step $3$ — ambiguous; on systems where 'void annotation' is an immediate UI action, this could be classified Y by the $30$-second test, but the audit-trail row that records the void itself is permanent and visible to anyone reading the chart history, so I classify N"* sta applicando il test in modo argomentativamente solido. **Accettare a Base o Buono se la conclusione finale è coerente** (la classificazione *N + dangerous* è quella che il workflow richiede operativamente); se la conclusione è *Y + safe*, è il discriminante che ferma il gate dove servirebbe — sotto Base.
- **Non confondere *clinical risk* con *reversibility* in C1.** Lo step $1$ (DB read) accede a PHI; uno studente potrebbe legittimamente argomentare che *"a read of PHI is a privacy event"* e quindi *"the agent should not read PHI unattended"*. Quella è una considerazione di **privacy governance**, **non** di reversibility. Il cap. $09$ insegna *reversibility*, non *privacy*. Lo studente che marca step $1$ come *irreversibile per PHI* sta confondendo i due assi (è il distrattore A di item $02$, qui in forma libera). Riportare allo studente che *privacy* e *reversibility* sono **due axes diversi** che vanno ragionati separatamente, e che la rubrica misura *reversibility*. **Sotto Base** per C1 se questa confusione blocca la corretta identificazione di step $3$ o step $4$.
- **Non premiare le "5 gate variants" o le "smart gate compositions" (ask + log + sandbox + ...).** L'item misura *placement*, non *gate architecture*. Uno studente che propone un *sandboxing* del EHR write o un *staging area* prima del SMS dispatch sta proponendo *engineering*, non *trust engineering* — è una risposta legittima ma a una *domanda diversa*. **Accettare la risposta come bonus** (è segno di competenza ingegneristica), **ma non sostituirla al placement della rubrica**: il C2 chiede *dove va il gate*, non *come è implementato*. Il livello Excellent della rubrica resta quello: *gate placement + render-and-wait + rule-survives-new-tool*.
- **Non penalizzare la mancanza della *giustificazione "rule survives new tool"* in C3 a Buono.** Quella è la *cifra di fluenza chapter-level* che porta a Excellent, **non** una soglia di Buono. Uno studente che giustifica i gate con la regola del cap. $09$ e include il *no-over-gating* di steps $1$-$2$ è già a Buono; il *survives-new-tool* è il passo *operativo* in più (e raro) che porta a Excellent.
