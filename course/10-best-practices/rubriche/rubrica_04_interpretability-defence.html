Rubric — Item 04 (Interpretability defence: technique + clinical argument)

Item: IB-10-best-practices-04 Objective: Match the interpretability technique to the model family — linear/logistic →\to coefficients; single tree →\to decision path; RF / gradient boosting →\to variable importance; neural network →\to SHAP or surrogate models — and weigh the interpretability/performance tradeoff as a clinical decision, not a technical one. State an operational rule that survives both audiences (clinical lead asking "why this patient?" and ethics committee asking "what does the model rely on?") (Obj. 3).

The rubric scores three criteria, one per component of Obj. 3, on three levels — Base (sufficiency floor), Good (solid command), Excellent (operational fluency that generalises beyond the specific scenario).

Criterion Base Good Excellent
C1 — Technique matching to model family (sub-q a) Names variable importance as the technique for the random forest, and does not propose reading off coefficients. The matching is correctly one-direction (this technique goes with this family). Below Base (critical): proposes "read off the random forest's coefficients" (coefficients exist for logistic regression, not for tree ensembles — the forest's prediction is the average of many trees, not a single per-feature linear weight); proposes "read the decision path of the random forest" (decision paths exist for a single tree, but a random forest is hundreds of trees, and no one path is the prediction); proposes a technique invented for a different family (e.g. "attention weights" — applies to Transformers, not to forests). These are the chapter's named mismatches; getting them wrong means the 1:11{:}1 matching has not been internalised. All of Base, plus the student names the global / per-patient distinction explicitly: variable importance is global (a population-level ranking — which features the ensemble relies on most across the data), so it answers "what does the model rely on?" but does not by itself answer "why this patient?". The student supplies an operational route for the per-patient question — minimally, "read the top-ranked features against this patient's chart values" (which is the chapter's pragmatic fall-back) — or any operationally-equivalent move. Naming the limit of variable importance and providing a per-patient route shows the student has not confused the technique with the explanation. All of Good, plus the student matches each component to the audience that needs it: global variable importance for the ethics committee (who ask population-level questions about which features the model uses, in order to judge whether the model is relying on something inappropriate — e.g. a proxy for socio-economic status); the per-patient route for the clinical lead (who will, on the next patient, need to explain to that specific patient — or to a colleague — why the model recommended what it recommended). Recognising that interpretability techniques have audiences — and that the choice of route is, in part, a choice about who is going to read the explanation — is the chapter-level fluency mark for the matching component of Obj. 3. Bonus accepted (not required) at Excellent: a student who additionally names a per-patient attribution technique seen outside the chapter's core (SHAP, LIME, partial dependence at the patient's values) is at Excellent if the citation is anchored operationally (the student names what the bonus technique adds over variable importance); a mechanical citation of the term without the anchor stays at Good.
C2 — Technical justification of the matching (sub-q a) States a correct technical reason for the matching, even if generic — e.g. "a random forest aggregates many trees, so there is no single weight per feature to read off", "variable importance ranks features by how much they contribute to the model's accuracy across the trees", "forests don't have a single equation, so a post-hoc ranking is needed". The reason is true and non-circular (the student does not assert the matching with no reason, and does not assert it for a wrong reason). Below Base (critical): the matching is asserted with no technical reason ("I'll use variable importance because it's standard in healthcare AI" — appeal to authority); the matching is asserted with a wrong technical reason ("random forests don't use coefficients because they overfit" — confuses interpretability with capacity: the forest doesn't have coefficients because it aggregates many trees, not because coefficients would be "too simple"; or "variable importance has lower bias than coefficients" / "variable importance is statistically more robust" — confuses interpretability with statistical-estimation properties, which are a different dimension entirely and are not what the chapter is asking about). All of Base, plus the student explicitly distinguishes built-in interpretability from post-hoc interpretability, and frames variable importance as the post-hoc technique that matches random forests because their structure (an average across many trees) does not expose a small set of human-readable parameters — there is nothing built-in to read off, so a post-hoc technique is required. The chapter's terminology of built-in vs post-hoc — or any operationally-equivalent formulation (read-from-the-model vs attribution-on-top-of-the-model) — is present. All of Good, plus recognition of the chapter's full 1:11{:}1 mapping as a rule, not a menu: the student notes (briefly, one sentence is enough) that linear / logistic →\to coefficients, single tree →\to decision path, RF / gradient boosting →\to variable importance, neural network →\to SHAP or surrogate models — and that the matching is determined by the structure each family exposes, not by the analyst's preference. The student understands that if tomorrow the team escalated further to a neural net, the technique would become SHAP or a surrogate model — the rule generalises across the model-family axis, and the student names that generalisation.
C3 — Clinical defensibility framing (sub-q b) The defence sentence gives a correct reason for preferring the chosen technique over reverting to logistic — but the reason is technical, with the model as the subject: "random forest reaches AUPRC 0.320.32 versus 0.270.27 for logistic", "we use variable importance because the model has no coefficients", "the performance gap justifies the loss of interpretability". The tradeoff is acknowledged, even if framed about the model rather than the patient. Below Base (critical): the defence sentence bypasses the tradeoff entirely — "interpretability is always better, so we should have used logistic" (refuses the question); "performance is always better, so the forest is fine" (refuses the question); the defence sentence is "I use variable importance because it's the standard" (appeal to authority, no tradeoff posed); the defence sentence is missing or merely restates the technical answer from (a) without a clinical reason. The chapter is explicit: "the tradeoff is a clinical decision, not a technical one" — bypassing the tradeoff means not having understood the objective. All of Base, plus the student names the tradeoff as a comparison: "we accept variable importance post-hoc over logistic built-in because the performance gap is meaningful enough to be worth the loss of native interpretability", or any operationally-equivalent formulation in which both sides of the tradeoff are named (what is gained: more readmissions caught; what is lost: built-in interpretation). The tradeoff is posed, but as a technical comparison between two model choices on technical criteria — the subject is still the model, not yet the patient. All of Good, plus the defence sentence inverts the subject from model to patient, names the cost-of-failure clinically, and positions the tradeoff as the clinical lead's call: the subject of the sentence is the patient ("the patient is better served by...", "on this audit, the patients we miss with logistic..."); the cost-of-failure is clinically qualified with at least one concrete clinical consequence (re-admission, possible adverse event, longer length of stay, ICU re-entry — not just "worse performance"); the clinical lead's signoff on the cost ratio is referenced (explicitly or implicitly) as the authority on which the tradeoff is being resolved — the data scientist applies the rule, the clinician sets the cost ratio. The sentence reads as something the clinical lead could read aloud back to the ethics committee without rephrasing — which is the operational test the chapter implicitly poses for clinical defensibility.

Soglia di sufficienza: tutti e tre C1, C2 e C3 a livello Base o superiore. Una proposta di "read off coefficients" su random forest in C1 è sotto Base indipendentemente dal resto (il matching tecnico è sbagliato; l'item misura matching, non eloquence). Un bypass del tradeoff in C3 ("interpretability is always better" / "performance is always better") è sotto Base indipendentemente dal resto (l'item misura applicare il tradeoff come scelta clinica, e bypassarlo significa non averlo applicato). Una mancanza di giustificazione tecnica in C2 (asserzione del matching senza ragione, o con ragione sbagliata) è sotto Base anche se il matching nominato è quello corretto — la rubrica misura capire perché, non recitare la parola variable importance.

Bias da evitare in correzione:

  • CRITICO — non premiare a Excellent una risposta che cita variable importance senza dire perché variable importance per questa famiglia. L'uso meccanico del termine è recall di parole, non applicazione della regola. Lo studente che scrive "I'll use variable importance because it is the standard interpretability technique for ensembles in clinical AI" sta facendo una appeal to authority travestita da matching: sotto Base su C2. Il livello GoodGood richiede built-in vs post-hoc; il livello ExcellentExcellent richiede full 1:11{:}1 mapping come regola. Senza una di queste due, variable importance è una parola, non una scelta.
  • CRITICO — non premiare a Excellent un framing che bypassa il tradeoff. Il chapter è esplicito sul punto: "the tradeoff is a clinical decision, not a technical one". Risposte come "interpretability is always better" (uso logistic always) o "performance is always better" (uso boxbox + post-hoc always) sono rifiuti del tradeoff, non sue risoluzioni. Sono sotto Base su C3 a prescindere dalla qualità retorica. Il Voce docente — non valutato di objectives.md (la nota sul gradient boosting vince empiricamente su dati clinici tabular) non ribalta questo punto: anche se gli ensembles vincono empiricamente, applicare la scelta richiede porre il tradeoff, non dichiararlo già risolto.
  • CRITICO — accettare a Excellent una risposta che inverte la scelta (revert to logistic + read off coefficients), se la mappatura tecnica è corretta E la giustificazione clinica è ben posta. Il chapter non dice "sempre il modello complesso"; dice "earn the complexity" — e l'opposto "defend the simplicity" è altrettanto valido se argomentato clinicamente. Uno studente che scrive "on this audit the extra recall the random forest buys is below the threshold our clinical lead has set for accepting model opacity — so we revert to logistic on 66 features, read off coefficients, and the model is built-in interpretable to the bedside" sta applicando la stessa rubrica con l'altro segno: matching tecnico corretto (logistic →\to coefficients, ✓\checkmark), tradeoff posto (gap nominato, soglia clinica nominata, ✓\checkmark), clinical-defensibility framing (cost-of-failure quantified vs cost-of-opacity quantified, ✓\checkmark). Accettare a Excellent. L'errore opposto — penalizzare la inversione di scelta per favorire random forest — sarebbe un bias da modello-vincente che la rubrica deve esplicitamente rifiutare.
  • SHAP / LIME / Shapley values come bonus, non come required. La mappatura ufficiale in objectives.md è RF/GB →\to variable importance e NN →\to SHAP/surrogate. Lo studente che applica la mappatura ufficiale (RF →\to variable importance) è al livello pieno della rubrica, non a un livello inferiore perché non ha "spinto" su SHAP. Uno studente che aggiunge SHAP / LIME / Shapley values / partial dependence come tecnica di per-patient attribution sopra la variable-importance globale è bonus a Excellent, e va riconosciuto come tale, se la citazione è ancorata operativamente (lo studente nomina cosa la tecnica bonus aggiunge su variable importance: la per-patient attribution che VI, essendo globale, non fornisce). Citazioni meccaniche ("perché è lo standard", "perché SHAP è interpretabile") non sono bonus — restano Good su C2 (terminologia senza matching). Da dove esce SHAP per uno studente che la cita? Tipicamente da: (i) ricerca per casa su Obj. 3 (lo studente curioso che ha letto un paper o un tutorial); (ii) ricordo della narrative illustration della prosa di narrative.md (Elena difende il modello con "SHAP on a forest"); (iii) menzione voce docente in classe di SHAP come upgrade della VI globale a per-patient — non valutata. In nessuno dei tre casi la mancata citazione è penalizzante.
  • Non penalizzare la mancanza di numeri esatti. L'Excellent può menzionare "meaningfully more readmissions caught" o "AUPRC 0.320.32 vs 0.270.27, an extra recall margin clinically material" — è equivalente. L'importante è che il guadagno sia nominato come clinicamente significativo, non che sia quantificato con la cifra esatta. Penalizzare uno studente per non aver tradotto 0.32−0.27=0.050.32 - 0.27 = 0.05 in termini di patients sarebbe un bias da quantitativismo che non è nello spirito di Obj. 3 (che chiede defensibility, non biostatistical precision).
  • Non premiare lo studente che cita Shapley values con dettagli matematici (cooperative game theory, marginal contributions, axiomatic uniqueness). Quei dettagli sono nel Voce docente — non valutato di objectives.md. Lo studente che li nomina mostra fluenza tecnica, ma la rubrica misura clinical defensibility, non SHAP theory. È bonus fuori rubrica — non sostituisce la giustificazione clinica.
  • Non confondere interpretability con explainability. Il chapter cita la distinzione concettuale (built-in interpretability vs post-hoc explainability) ma non la valuta ("la cito ma non la valuto" — objectives.md). Uno studente che usa interpretability in entrambi i sensi senza distinguere è a livello Good, non penalizzato; uno studente che distingue è bonus fuori rubrica, non necessario per Excellent. Il discriminante di Excellent è il framing clinico, non la precisione terminologica AI/ML.
  • Variable importance è agnostico fra le sue varianti operative. Esistono almeno due implementazioni di variable importance per random forest: Mean Decrease in Impurity (MDI — la versione built-in di scikit-learn .feature_importances_, calcolata come riduzione totale di impurità accumulata a ogni split su ogni feature) e permutation importance (più robusta sui feature correlati, calcolata permutando ogni feature sul validation set e misurando il calo di performance). Lo studente che cita una variante specifica ("MDI from scikit-learn's feature_importances_" o "permutation importance with kk repeats on the validation split") mostra fluenza tecnica ma non è privilegiato rispetto allo studente che cita variable importance senza specifica. Distinguere MDI da permutation è bonus fuori rubrica, come SHAP / LIME / Shapley values. La rubrica misura matching + clinical defensibility, non implementation choice.
  • Non premiare a Excellent un'inversione senza il tradeoff posto. Il punto della clausola sopra (accettare l'inversione) è che l'inversione DEVE essere argomentata; un revert silenzioso a logistic ("I prefer logistic because it's simpler") non è Excellent — è Base su C1 e sotto Base su C3 (il tradeoff non è stato posto). L'inversione è una scelta clinica legittima se argomentata clinicamente, non una scappatoia "the simpler model is always safer".
