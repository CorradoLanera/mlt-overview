Item 03 — Count the cost: 0/1 risk on a sample

ID: IB-02-classifiers-03 Learning objective: Apply the 0/1 loss to a classifier's predictions and interpret its risk on a sample as the average 0/1 loss over the patients — the quantity training seeks to minimise (Obj. 3). Category: Application Type: MCQ (scenario) Difficulty: medium Chapter: 02-classifiers

Question

A binary classifier ff is tested on 10 patients. For each patient the table reports the true class YY and the classifier's prediction Ŷ=f(X)\hat Y = f(X):

Patient True YY Predicted Ŷ=f(X)\hat Y = f(X)
1 high-risk high-risk
2 low-risk low-risk
3 high-risk low-risk
4 low-risk low-risk
5 high-risk high-risk
6 low-risk high-risk
7 high-risk high-risk
8 low-risk low-risk
9 high-risk low-risk
10 low-risk low-risk

Using the 0/1 loss 𝔏(y,ŷ)=1\mathfrak{L}(y, \hat y) = 1 if y≠ŷy \ne \hat y and 00 otherwise, what is the classifier's 0/1 risk on this sample — the average 0/1 loss over the 10 patients?

Options

  • A) 0/100/10
  • B) 2/102/10
  • C) 3/103/10
  • D) 7/107/10

Expected answer

Correct: C (3/103/10). Going row by row, the 0/1 loss is 11 exactly when Y≠ŶY \ne \hat Y. Three patients are misclassified: #3 (true high-risk, predicted low-risk), #6 (true low-risk, predicted high-risk), and #9 (true high-risk, predicted low-risk). The average 0/1 loss is therefore

risk on sample(f)=110∑i=110𝔏(yi,ŷi)=310.\text{risk on sample}(f) = \frac{1}{10} \sum_{i=1}^{10} \mathfrak{L}(y_i, \hat y_i) = \frac{3}{10}.

Why the distractors are plausible but wrong:

  • A (0/100/10) is the "the classifier looks accurate, so the risk must be zero" misreading: it confuses most-patients-right with no-error-on-any-patient. Risk counts the misclassifications, and there are three.
  • B (2/102/10) counts only false negatives — the two high-risk patients (#3\#3 and #9\#9) that the classifier missed — and ignores the false positive #6\#6 (a low-risk patient flagged as high-risk). The 0/1 loss is symmetric: every disagreement costs 11, regardless of which direction the error goes.
  • D (7/107/10) is the accuracy / risk inversion: 77 is the count of correctly classified patients (10−3=710 - 3 = 7), so 7/107/10 is the accuracy, not the risk. The 0/1 risk is the error rate, not the success rate — the very point of the chapter is that we are counting costs, not wins.
