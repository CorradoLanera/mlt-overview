Item 02 — Partition the plane

ID: IB-02-classifiers-02 Learning objective: Represent a classifier geometrically as a function f:ℝd→{1,…,K}f:\mathbb{R}^d \to \{1,\ldots,K\} that partitions the feature space into decision regions (one region per class) (Obj. 2). Category: Application Type: applicativa (open-ended applicative) Difficulty: medium Chapter: 02-classifiers

Question

A clinical screening tool uses two patient features — fasting glucose xx (mg/dL) and BMI yy (kg/m²) — to flag patients as high-risk (11) or low-risk (00). The screening tool is the classifier f:ℝ2→{0,1}f:\mathbb{R}^2 \to \{0, 1\} defined by the rule

f(x,y)={1if x+2y>2000otherwise.f(x, y) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x + 2y > 200 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}

Three new patients arrive:

Patient Fasting glucose xx BMI yy
P1 100100 3030
P2 140140 3535
P3 9090 6060

Answer the three sub-questions:

  1. Compute ff for each of P1, P2, P3 — i.e., assign each patient the label high-risk or low-risk — showing the arithmetic.
  2. Sketch or describe the two decision regions of ff on the (x,y)(x, y) plane: draw the boundary line x+2y=200x + 2y = 200 and indicate which side is the high-risk region (f=1f = 1) and which is the low-risk region (f=0f = 0).
  3. Explain why the two decision regions together cover the entire plane ℝ2\mathbb{R}^2 with no gaps and no overlaps, and why this is a necessary property of any classifier f:ℝ2→{0,1}f:\mathbb{R}^2 \to \{0, 1\} — not a stylistic choice of how to draw it.

Expected answer

  1. Compute x+2yx + 2y for each patient and compare with 200200:

    • P1: 100+2⋅30=160<200100 + 2 \cdot 30 = 160 < 200 → f(P1)=0f(\text{P1}) = 0 → low-risk.
    • P2: 140+2⋅35=210>200140 + 2 \cdot 35 = 210 > 200 → f(P2)=1f(\text{P2}) = 1 → high-risk.
    • P3: 90+2⋅60=210>20090 + 2 \cdot 60 = 210 > 200 → f(P3)=1f(\text{P3}) = 1 → high-risk.

  2. Boundary: the line x+2y=200x + 2y = 200 passes through (200,0)(200, 0) and (0,100)(0, 100). The plane splits into two half-planes:

    • High-risk region (f=1f = 1): the half-plane above-right of the line, where x+2y>200x + 2y > 200. P2 and P3 fall here.
    • Low-risk region (f=0f = 0): the half-plane below-left of the line, where x+2y<200x + 2y < 200. P1 falls here.
    • (The boundary line itself goes to f=0f = 0, per the rule's strict inequality.)

  3. The regions cover ℝ2\mathbb{R}^2 with no gaps, no overlaps because ff is a function: by definition, for every input (x,y)∈ℝ2(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2, ff must return exactly one output in {0,1}\{0, 1\} — never none (which would be a gap, an input with no label) and never two (which would be an overlap, an input with conflicting labels). The two decision regions are exactly the preimages f−1(0)={(x,y):f(x,y)=0}f^{-1}(0) = \{(x,y) : f(x,y) = 0\} and f−1(1)={(x,y):f(x,y)=1}f^{-1}(1) = \{(x,y) : f(x,y) = 1\}; their union is the whole plane, and their intersection is empty. Saying "ff is a classifier" is saying "the regions partition ℝ2\mathbb{R}^2" — the geometric picture and the function definition are the same statement.
