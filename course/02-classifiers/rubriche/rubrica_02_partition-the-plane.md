# Rubric — Item 02 (Partition the plane)

**Item**: IB-02-classifiers-02
**Learning objective**: Represent a classifier geometrically as a function $f:\mathbb{R}^d \to \{1,\ldots,K\}$ that partitions the feature space into decision regions (Obj. 2).

| Criterion | Base | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — Compute labels for the three patients** (sub-question 1) | **1 of 3** labels correct, *and* the arithmetic ($x + 2y$) is shown for at least one patient — student is applying the rule, not guessing. | **2 of 3** labels correct *and* arithmetic shown for each computed patient; an incorrect label is traceable to an arithmetic slip, not a misuse of the rule. | **3 of 3** labels correct, arithmetic shown for each, and the boundary case (strict vs non-strict inequality) is handled consistently with the stated rule. |
| **C2 — Geometric representation of the partition** (sub-question 2) | A boundary is drawn *or* described, and the two regions are placed on *opposite* sides of it — but the assignment of which side is $f = 1$ vs $f = 0$ is missing, ambiguous, or inconsistent with the labels computed in sub-question 1. | The boundary line $x + 2y = 200$ is correct (passes through $(200, 0)$ and $(0, 100)$, or equivalently described) *and* the two half-planes are correctly tagged: above-right = $f = 1$, below-left = $f = 0$, consistent with sub-question 1. | All of Good, plus the boundary line itself is unambiguously assigned (here: to $f = 0$, per the strict inequality in the rule), and the three computed patients are placed in their correct regions on the sketch / description. |
| **C3 — Justify "no gaps, no overlaps" as a necessary property** (sub-question 3) | Student asserts that the two regions cover the plane without overlapping, but the *reason* is given as a property of *this particular* classifier (e.g., "because the line splits the plane in two") — not as a property of *any* classifier. | Student names the underlying reason — "every point in the plane gets a label" / "the regions are determined by $f$, which assigns one label to each input" — but without explicitly invoking the **function** property of $f$. | Student explicitly invokes the **function** property: *because $f$ is a function, every input $(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ has exactly one image in $\{0,1\}$ — never zero (gap), never two (overlap)*. Bonus: identifies the two regions as the preimages $f^{-1}(0)$ and $f^{-1}(1)$ whose union is $\mathbb{R}^2$ and intersection is empty. |

**Soglia di sufficienza:** **almeno 2 criteri su 3 a livello *Base*** (e nessuno sotto Base). Below-Base in C1 = $0/3$ labels correct *or* no arithmetic shown anywhere; below-Base in C2 = no boundary drawn *or* described, or the boundary contradicts the rule entirely; below-Base in C3 = no attempt at justifying the no-gap-no-overlap property (the sub-question is the conceptual gem — leaving it blank is not "Base").

**Bias da evitare in correzione:**

- **Premiare la sketch "bella" rispetto alla sketch corretta.** Per C2 conta solo *l'orientamento corretto della retta + corretta etichettatura delle due regioni*; uno schizzo a mano libera con righe e descrizione testuale vale quanto un grafico pulito.
- **Penalizzare il lessico non disciplinare in C3 se la sostanza è corretta.** Se lo studente scrive "every point of the plane has to land in one and only one region because $f$ gives exactly one answer per input" senza usare la parola *preimage* o la notazione $f^{-1}$, è comunque **Excellent**: ha colto il punto. Il livello Excellent premia la *connessione esplicita alla proprietà di funzione*, non la notazione formale.
- **Penalizzare la confusione strict-vs-non-strict in C1/C2 oltre il dovuto.** L'assegnazione del confine $(x+2y=200)$ a $f=0$ vs $f=1$ è dettata dalla regola data (strict $>$); confonderla è un errore di lettura della consegna, non un errore concettuale sull'Obj. 2 — vale al più una nota, non un downgrade da Good a Base.
- **Penalizzare l'aritmetica sbagliata in C1 se il metodo è corretto.** Uno studente che computa $f(\text{P3}) = f(90, 60)$ come "$90 + 60 = 150 < 200 → 0$" (dimentica il coefficiente $2$) ha sbagliato l'aritmetica, non l'applicazione della partizione: due labels su tre corrette con l'aritmetica visibile resta **Good**.
