Internal coherence — the three items together test the chapter's three-piece kit

The chapter's narrative closes on what Elena calls "the chapter's three-piece kit" (narrative.md): a way to ask (the seven-slot prompt), a way to say no (the competence rule), and a way to deliver (channel choice + token unit). The three items map one-to-one onto those three pieces:

  • Item 01 tests the way to ask — but with a structural twist: the summative tested the most load-bearing slots; the item tests least load-bearing too, and the three slots (role / style / examples) the summative leaves implicit. A student who has only done the summative cannot solve item 01 by recall.
  • Item 02 tests the way to say no — and pushes it past the simple form (delegate only what you can check) into the special case the chapter's narrative reserves for its last paragraph (delegating well is not delegating everything). Scenario B is the textbook instance: the LLM may get the answer right, but checking requires the database that should have been the original source. A student who marks B as delegable has read the rule but missed its higher-order form.
  • Item 03 tests the way to deliver — and surfaces the disjunctive reading of the API-trigger rule that is easy to miss. The chapter states the rule with three conditions (volume, repeatability, integration) and any one suffices; option B in the MCQ tempts a student to read all three as required. Picking C requires the disjunctive reading the chapter teaches but does not over-emphasise.

Together, the three items verify the chapter's practice rather than its vocabulary. A student who passes all three has demonstrated the three-piece kit on a new task (surgical-procedure extraction), with a new normative test (the not-delegable-regardless-of-expertise case), at a new channel-rule discrimination (recurrence flips the rule). The summative's 200200 ED letters scenario is recalled in none of them — by design.

Anti-leakage — vocabulary boundary with ch. 09+

The items stay strictly within ch. 08 vocabulary: prompt, role / context / task / instructions / output / style / examples (the seven slots), competence rule, delegate, failure mode, expertise, Web chat / Prompt design / API (the three channels), token (with the ≈34\approx \dfrac{3}{4}-word rule of thumb). They do not presuppose any concept from ch. 09+ — no agent, no tool, no control loop, no decision points, no trust boundary, no reversibility, no human-in-the-loop, no autonomous search / ask for interaction / ask for sharing / do stuff (the four agent interaction modes), no gate (the spatial reformulation of the check that ch. 09 will introduce). The chapter's pre-hook to ch. 09 (Wednesday afternoon, the four-step PubMed-summarise-draft-email workflow, the trust gate) is staged in the chapter's narrative and storyboard frame 6, not inside the items.

The references in item 03's expected-answer to the chapter's three triggers for API (volume / repeatability / integration), and the references in item 01 to role / style / examples as named slots, are not anti-leakage violations: those terms are ch. 08 vocabulary as introduced in Obj. 1 and Obj. 3 of objectives.md.

Pre-hook to ch. 09 — left to the chapter, not embedded in items

Same convention as ch. 04 → ch. 05, ch. 05 → ch. 06, and ch. 07 → ch. 08: the items measure what this chapter has taught, with no forward references; the narrative and storyboard of the chapter carry the bridge to the next one.
