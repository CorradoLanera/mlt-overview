08 — Using ChatGPT in practice · Learning objectives & summative exercise

Chapter: Using ChatGPT in practice · 15 min · biomedical/clinical graduate students (UBEP). Student-facing text in English; teacher design notes (nota docente) in Italian.

Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, students can:

  1. Construct a structured prompt for a given clinical task using the 7-slot template — role (who the model is impersonating), context (the situation the task lives in), task (the one verb the model must perform), instructions (the procedure to follow), output (the exact shape of the deliverable), style (constraints on tone, format, what not to add), examples (1–3 worked input/output pairs) — and justify in one line, for each slot they used, why that slot is load-bearing for the task at hand (i.e. which failure mode is suppressed by that slot).
  2. State and apply the competence rule: only delegate to an LLM tasks whose answer you can independently judge as correct or wrong. Concretely: given a candidate task, identify (a) a plausible-looking failure mode the model could produce (e.g. a fabricated citation, a wrong dosage rounded to a plausible number, a hallucinated guideline), and (b) the expertise (clinical / methodological / statistical) the user must already have to catch that failure. If the user does not have that expertise, the task is not delegable.
  3. Choose the right access mode (Web chat / Prompt design / API) for a given workflow and estimate its token cost using the rule of thumb 11 token ≈34\approx \dfrac{3}{4} word (i.e. ∼750\sim 750 words ≈1000\approx 1\,000 tokens, ∼750000\sim 750\,000 words ≈106\approx 10^6 tokens). State the decision rule: Web for ad-hoc one-shot questions, Prompt design (still in chat) for a small batch of repeated structured tasks done by hand, API for any workflow that must be repeatable, integrated into other software, or applied at volume (≳100\gtrsim 100 items).

Nota docente: tre obiettivi per 15′ — il limite. L'arco di ch 08 non è "addestrare al prompt engineering avanzato": in 15′ si stabilisce il minimo operativo perché lo studente UBEP, lunedì mattina, sappia (a) come strutturare una richiesta (Obj. 1), (b) quando non chiederla affatto (Obj. 2 — la regola della competenza), e (c) quale strumento scegliere in base al volume del lavoro (Obj. 3). Obj. 2 è il cuore etico/operativo del capitolo e va segnalato come non opzionale: tutto il resto del corso può andar bene tecnicamente, ma se uno studente usa un LLM per qualcosa che non sa giudicare, il corso ha fallito. Obj. 3 collega il prompt design al come lo deploymento (web vs API) e introduce i token come unità di costo — non come dettaglio tecnico. Voce docente — non valutato: chain-of-thought prompting, few-shot vs zero-shot vs many-shot, system vs user vs developer roles nell'API OpenAI, function calling / tool use (è il pre-hook di ch 09 e va in narrative.md), structured output (JSON schema, response_format), embedding e retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), temperature/top-p, context window e i prezzi per modello specifico, privacy / GDPR / data residency su chat.openai.com (cita ma non valutare). Il pre-hook a ch 09 ("finora ogni risposta del modello passa dai tuoi occhi prima di diventare un'azione — ma cosa succede quando il modello agisce prima che tu controlli?") va in narrative.md, non qui.

Summative live exercise — "Monday morning: rank the ED letters"

Nota docente: sommativo, gira negli ultimi ~7′ (min 8–15) dopo i tre livelli (Web / Prompt design / API) e dopo la slide sui token. Banco unico, condiviso da tutta la classe (presenza + remoto): uno scenario clinico realistico proiettato e stampato —

Scenario: You are a resident in the Emergency Department. Your senior asks you to triage 200200 anonymised ED discharge letters from last month into two categories — "trauma-related" vs "non-trauma" — to feed a quality-improvement audit. Each letter is on average ∼300\sim 300 words.

A fianco, una scheda prompt con i 7 slot già etichettati (vuoti), due blanks etichettati Failure mode: ___ e Expertise needed to catch it: ___, un mini-calcolatore di token (rule of thumb 11 token ≈3/4\approx 3/4 word, da applicare a 200×300200 \times 300 parole), e un box "tre modi" con tre celle vuote da spuntare e da motivare in una riga: Web chat / Prompt design (chat, a mano) / API.

Field Content
Objective verified Obj. 1 (7-slot prompt + justification) — verified in sub-task (i). Obj. 2 (competence rule) — verified in sub-task (ii), the what-you-cannot-catch identification. Obj. 3 (access mode + token estimate) — verified in sub-tasks (iii) and (iv).
In-person action Table groups receive the scenario + the empty 7-slot card + the token calculator + the "tre modi" box. They: (i) fill the 7-slot prompt for the trauma/non-trauma triage task (e.g. role = "You are a clinical-coding assistant"; context = "You are reading ED discharge letters from a teaching hospital, the goal is an internal quality audit"; task = "Classify each letter as trauma or non-trauma"; instructions = "Read the chief complaint and the mechanism-of-injury fields; if either names an external traumatic event, classify trauma"; output = "Return only one token: 'trauma' or 'non-trauma'"; style = "No explanation, no extra words"; examples = two short worked cases) — and circle exactly two slots that are most load-bearing here: task and output with one phrase each justifying why (the other five are commented in the reveal, not live); (ii) answer in two labelled blanks on the card (Failure mode: ___ / Expertise needed to catch it: ___): "Name one plausible-looking failure of this LLM call that a non-clinician would NOT catch, and the clinical expertise needed to catch it." — expected pattern: e.g. "a letter describing a fall in a 92-yo with osteoporosis classified as 'non-trauma' because the mechanism is described in geriatric/fragility framing — only a clinician knows fragility falls are trauma", the expertise: clinical knowledge of fragility-fracture epidemiology; (iii) estimate the token cost on the calculator: 200 letters×300 words=60000 words200 \text{ letters} \times 300 \text{ words} = 60\,000 \text{ words}, and 60000×43=8000060\,000 \times \dfrac{4}{3} = 80\,000 tokens of input, +200+ 200 tokens of output (one short label per letter) ≈80000\approx 80\,000 tokens total; (iv) circle the access mode in the "tre modi" box (the correct answer is API — 200200 items is at the limit where copy-paste in chat stops being practical, and an audit needs repeatability), with one phrase justifying why not Web (you would copy-paste 200200 times, no traceability) and why not Prompt design alone (it is the right prompt, delivered through the wrong channel). Each group photographs its card and drops it into the shared doc.
Remote equivalent Same scenario + same 7-slot card + same token calculator + same "tre modi" box on a shared doc. Remote groups type the seven slots directly into their slot, type the 1-sentence failure mode, type the token calculation, and type the access mode + justification. Two Slido polls run live, capturing all students individually: Poll 1 — "Token estimate for the input (200 letters × 300 words)" (options ∼60000\sim 60\,000 / ∼80000\sim 80\,000 / ∼200000\sim 200\,000 / ∼600000\sim 600\,000); Poll 2 — "Best access mode for this task" (options Web chat / Prompt design in chat / API / "do it by hand without an LLM"). The polls make the remote signal on Obj. 3 (token estimate + mode) visible next to the in-person sheets.
Shared artifact A single shared doc / whiteboard with one row per group containing: the seven filled slots · two circled load-bearing slots (task, output) + 1-phrase justification each · Failure mode + Expertise blanks · token estimate (calculation shown, not just the number) · circled access mode + 1-phrase why-not-the-others. The two Slido bar charts (Poll 1 and Poll 2) are projected next to it, visible to in-person and remote at once.
Timing 7 min, minutes 8–15 (end of chapter): ~1′ read the scenario + open the polls, ~2′30″ fill the 7-slot prompt + circle task and output with 1-phrase justification each (Poll 1 closes at min 11 or at ≥80%\geq 80\% votes, whichever is later; hard cap 9090″), ~1′ fill the Failure mode / Expertise blanks, ~1′ token estimate + access mode circled (Poll 2 closes at min 13 or at ≥80%\geq 80\%, same floor), ~1′30″ instructor-led reveal — a model 7-slot prompt is read off the board (one group's is taken as the canonical example), Poll 1 histogram is shown (the wrong distractors are named: 6000060\,000 = "ha calcolato in parole non in token — è il segnale che ha letto la slide ma non ha applicato la conversione ×4/3\times 4/3", 200000200\,000 = "ha confuso il prompt template con il whole context window, ha contato il prompt per ogni lettera (cioè ha contato ∼1000\sim 1000 tokens di prompt ×200\times 200 chiamate, mentre il sistema template è una stringa unica)", 600000600\,000 = "ha confuso parole con caratteri o ha applicato ×10\times 10 — verifica il senso degli ordini di grandezza"), Poll 2 histogram is shown (the wrong distractors are named: Web chat = "ha sottostimato 200200 — provi a fare 200200 copy-paste in chat e poi mi dica", Prompt design in chat = "ha confuso avere un buon prompt (sì) con consegnarlo manualmente 200200 volte (no, ti serve l'API)", "do it by hand" = "è una risposta seria — per 2020 lettere sì, per 200200 no, ed è esattamente la soglia che Obj. 3 ti chiede di sentire"), and the instructor explicitly closes the chapter's cliff-hanger with the spine beat to ch 09: "in this exercise you wrote the prompt, you delivered it, and you would read every one of the 200200 outputs before doing anything with them. That is chat. But what if the system acts on the output without you reading every one? That is an agent — and that is ch 09."
Success criterion The class produces, on the shared artifact: (i) all seven slots filled (post-hoc quality check on the photographed artifact, not a live criterion), and the two circled slots are task and output, each with a 1-phrase justification that names the failure mode it suppresses (live criterion); (ii) the 1-sentence failure mode names (a) a clinically-plausible, hard-to-spot error (not "the model invents nonsense" — the model invents plausible nonsense, that is the dangerous mode), and (b) the specific clinical expertise needed to catch it; (iii) the token estimate shows the calculation, not just the number — 200×300×43≈80000200 \times 300 \times \dfrac{4}{3} \approx 80\,000 tokens of input, with output negligible; Poll 1 shows the modal answer at ∼80000\sim 80\,000; (iv) the access mode circled is API, with the justification naming repeatability / integration / volume (any of the three) — Poll 2 shows the modal answer at API. Live signal of mastery: the class can read off the board, without prompting, that a usable Monday-morning LLM workflow is one part prompt-engineering, one part competence-honesty, and one part choosing the channel that matches the volume — and that for 200200 letters of an audit the answer is API, not chat.

Nota docente — perché duale funziona qui: l'artefatto è uno solo (lo scenario + scheda 7-slot + calcolatore + box "tre modi"), tutto il sommativo è cella-da-riempire + frase singola + calcolo numerico + check su una opzione — niente da disegnare ex novo, niente asimmetria presenza/remoto. I due poll Slido danno il segnale immediato e individuale sui due punti più diagnosticabili (errore di unità di misura su token; errore di scelta del canale). Lo scenario è clinicamente plausibile, gestibile in 1515 min lordi, e non richiede prerequisiti tecnici NLP — il sommativo verifica operatività, non teoria.

Nota docente — sulla scelta dello scenario e dei distrattori: "200200 lettere ED, trauma vs non-trauma" è scelto perché (a) plausibile in un audit reale di qualità ospedaliera; (b) al confine 2020–200200: 2020 → copy-paste in chat va bene, 200200 → no, e voglio che lo studente senta dove sta la soglia; (c) fragility-fracture failure mode è un esempio reale e celebre di errore di triage automatico (le cadute geriatriche sono trauma ma vengono spesso classificate diversamente). I distrattori del Poll 1 sono diagnostici: 6000060\,000 = parole anziché token (errore di unità); 200000200\,000 = ha duplicato il prompt template per ogni chiamata (concettualmente OK ma non rilevante qui — premia l'osservazione e correggi: il template è una stringa unica nella system role); 600000600\,000 = errore di ordine di grandezza, va segnalato come campanello d'allarme — quando un risultato è 10×10\times off, non aggiustare il numero, verifica il modello mentale. I distrattori del Poll 2 coprono i tre archetipi sbagliati: Web chat (non sa il volume), Prompt design in chat (sa il prompt, non sa la delivery), do it by hand (risposta seria — premia chi la dà perché dimostra senso del costo).

Nota docente — anti-trabocchetto su "ma posso anche usare la grep, no?": uno studente attento può chiedere se per "trauma vs non-trauma" basterebbe un keyword-search (grep su "trauma|fall|injury|MVA|..."). La risposta — voce docente nel reveal, non parte della valutazione — è: sì, e dovresti chiederti se ti basta. Se l'audit è di alto livello (volume) e la qualità della classificazione conta poco, una grep ben fatta vince per costo/replicabilità. Se l'audit deve essere clinicamente difendibile, l'LLM con il giusto prompt fa meglio sui casi al margine (es. fragility fall descritto in linguaggio non standard) — ma allora ti serve un essere umano che validi un sample, e il costo torna. Premia lo studente che fa l'osservazione: è il livello di pragmatismo che il corso vuole costruire — l'LLM non è sempre la risposta, anche quando l'LLM è quello che hai imparato.
